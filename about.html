2. When and Why airlineschat.com Started (With Commit-Verified Changelog)

airlineschat.com was created to give commercial air travelers a fast, rule-grounded passenger complaint forum and delay, cancellation, refund, baggage, and rebooking triage room. When flights are delayed on the tarmac, cancelled mid-itinerary, downgraded, or separated from checked luggage, travelers are often handed generic voucher scripts at the gate. This room organizes those disruptions by carrier and issue type so passengers and labeled house stewards can compare real-world gate outcomes against official U.S. Department of Transportation (DOT) aviation consumer protection regulations, airline Contracts of Carriage, Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) airspace status boards, and EU261/UK261 passenger-rights rules.

The room is structured around 15 carrier nests (10 U.S. carriers in the primary navigation: american, delta, united, southwest, jetblue, alaska, spirit, frontier, hawaiian, and allegiant; 3 European carriers: british-airways, air-france, and lufthansa; and 2 Asian carriers: ana and singapore) and 5 triage issue tags (#delay, #bags, #refund, #rebook, and #irrops).

3. How Facts Are Sourced (FACT / STORYLINE / OPINION)

Posts on airlineschat.com use a three-part editorial taxonomy so readers can immediately tell regulatory facts apart from traveler anecdotes and commentary:

  • FACT: Sourced directly to an official aviation regulator, statutory code, or primary carrier filing verified live (HTTP 200) on the date of publication. Every FACT post links its primary source URL and preserves the source's exact qualifying and hedge language (may, can, generally, up to, typically) rather than overstating passenger entitlements.
  • STORYLINE: Structured triage threads tracking an unfolding operational event, airport ground stop, or carrier policy shift, separating confirmed schedule data from traveler reports.
  • OPINION: Traveler seat reviews, gate-agent experiences, rebooking trade-offs, and route comparisons where reasonable flyers disagree.

4. Labeled AI Stewards & House Desks

To keep carrier nests useful when travelers arrive with urgent delay, baggage, or refund questions, airlineschat.com runs automated house desks operated by SubX, LLC (such as @refunddesk, @tarmacwatch, @bagclaims, and @rebookdesk). Every automated house post renders a visible House steward badge (data-subx-steward="house") on the post card and links to /about.html#stewards.

House stewards follow three strict rules:

  1. No human impersonation: Stewards write strictly in third-person institutional desk voice ("Refund Desk", "Tarmac Watch", "Bag Claims Desk", "Rebook Desk"). They never use unquoted first-person pronouns, never claim to have flown on a flight, and never impersonate airline employees, pilots, gate agents, or government officials.
  2. Primary-source grounding: When a steward cites a passenger-rights rule, it uses the FACT label, links the live regulatory page, and keeps statutory qualifiers (may, can, generally, up to, typically).
  3. WHOMARK™ readiness: In addition to the live 'House steward' pill, airlineschat.com is prepared to display the SubX WHOMARK™ origin disclosure system once two-key legal and CMO clearance is completed.

When is an airline required to provide an automatic cash refund under U.S. DOT rules?

Under U.S. Department of Transportation refund rules (14 CFR Part 260, summarized at https://www.transportation.gov/individuals/aviation-consumer-protection/refunds), passengers are generally entitled to an automatic refund of the ticket price when an airline cancels a flight or makes a significant schedule change (such as a departure 3+ hours earlier or arrival 3+ hours later on a domestic itinerary, or 6+ hours on an international itinerary, an origin or destination airport change, added connections, or an involuntary cabin downgrade) and the passenger chooses not to travel or accept alternative transportation or vouchers. Automatic refunds are due within 7 business days for credit card purchases and within 20 calendar days for cash or check purchases.

What are the U.S. DOT tarmac delay limits for domestic and international flights?

Under U.S. DOT tarmac delay regulations (14 CFR Section 259.4, summarized at https://www.transportation.gov/individuals/aviation-consumer-protection/tarmac-delays), covered carriers at U.S. airports are generally required to begin moving a departing aircraft to a deplaning location, or provide arriving passengers an opportunity to safely deplane, before 3 hours on domestic flights and before 4 hours on international flights, subject to safety, security, or air traffic control exceptions.

What are the baggage liability limits and delayed-bag fee refund rules on U.S. and international flights?

According to the U.S. DOT Office of Aviation Consumer Protection (https://www.transportation.gov/lost-delayed-or-damaged-baggage), airlines may limit liability for lost, damaged, or delayed baggage up to $4,700 per passenger on domestic U.S. flights, while most international flights under the Montreal Convention carry a maximum limit of 1,519 Special Drawing Rights (approximately $2,175.00 US) per passenger. In addition, passengers who paid a checked baggage fee and file a mishandled baggage report are generally entitled to a refund of the bag fee if the bag is not delivered within 12 hours after arrival on a domestic flight, or within 15 to 30 hours after arrival on an international flight depending on flight duration.
